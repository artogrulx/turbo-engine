# Open Meadow

A living voxel meadow you can explore, dig and build in — right in the browser. Rolling hills, rivers and lakes, drifting patches of wildflowers, bees and butterflies, and water that actually flows.

**Play it:** https://artogrulx.github.io/turbo-engine/

No install, no build step, no image or sound files: everything (textures, icons, audio) is generated by the code at start-up.

## Run it locally

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder and visit it:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
# then open http://localhost:8000
```

You need a recent desktop browser with WebGL2 and an internet connection on first load (Three.js r149 is fetched from jsDelivr, with unpkg as a fallback). It was developed and tested in Chromium-based browsers; Firefox and Safari have not been tested.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `W` `A` `S` `D` | Walk |
| `Shift` (or double-tap `W`) | Sprint |
| `Space` | Jump · swim up · climb out of water |
| `C` | Crouch (you won't walk off edges) · dive |
| Mouse | Look around (arrow keys also work) |
| Left click | Hold to mine a block · click to pick a flower |
| Right click | Place a block or flower · use the bucket |
| Middle click | Pick the block you are looking at |
| `1`–`9` · mouse wheel | Choose hotbar slot |
| `E` | Open / close the inventory |
| `Esc` | Pause and release the mouse |
| `F3` | Debug information |

The inventory supports click to pick up / place, right-click to split a stack, `Shift`+click to move a stack between storage and hotbar, hovering a slot and pressing `1`–`9` to swap it into the hotbar, a Sort button and a trash slot. An empty bucket scoops up a still water source; a full one pours a new source.

## What's in the world

- **Terrain** — meadows, wooded hills with ridges and bare rock on the steep slopes, river valleys, lakes, and sand / gravel / clay shores, generated in 16×16×128 chunks that stream in and out around you. You spawn in an open meadow, never in a tree or underwater.
- **Vegetation** — oak, birch, big branching oaks and highland spruce, bushes, boulders, coal and iron ore, ferns, cattails, mushrooms, tall grass and ten kinds of flower (poppy, dandelion, cornflower, oxeye daisy, allium, orange and pink tulips, lavender, buttercup, bluebell) laid out in drifting patches of one palette, so meadows bloom in drifts rather than random single flowers. Grass and flowers sway in the wind; pollen drifts through the air.
- **Wildlife** — up to 30 butterflies (six species) and 36 bees. They pick flowers, settle, hover and feed; butterflies scatter when you get close, bees dart between blooms and sometimes come to look at you. They fade away at night.
- **Water** — a real simulation on the block grid: sources, flowing levels and falling water. It runs downhill toward drops, fills holes you dig beside a lake, forms waterfalls, and drains when its source is removed. The surface is drawn as one smooth animated sheet with depth-based colour and transparency, flow animation and foam.
- **Swimming** — buoyancy, drag, currents that push you, underwater fog and caustics, muffled sound, and a breath meter.
- **Sky** — a 24-minute day/night cycle with sun, moon, stars, drifting clouds, golden-hour colours and shadows that follow you.
- **Physics** — axis-aligned bounding-box collision at a fixed 120 Hz, so you can't clip into blocks; crouch edge protection, optional auto-jump, fall damage and health regeneration.
- **Mining and building** — hardness-based mining with a crack overlay and break particles. Mined blocks leave real holes you can fall into and the ground is never restored; placing checks that the spot is free and that the block wouldn't overlap you. Edits are kept per chunk, so they survive chunks unloading and reloading.

## Settings and saving

Settings cover render distance (3–12 chunks), shadows, vegetation density, resolution scale, detailed leaves, mouse sensitivity, invert Y, field of view, auto-jump, view bobbing, the day/night cycle and volume.

Your world edits, inventory, position, time of day and settings are stored in the browser's `localStorage`. The game autosaves every minute and whenever you pause or quit; **New World** starts a fresh seed. Clearing site data erases the save.

## Project layout

```
index.html        page structure: loading, title, pause, settings, inventory and HUD
style.css         all UI styling
main.js           boot, the game state machine, pointer lock, input, HUD, settings, saving, main loop
inventory.js      inventory model and its UI
engine/
  noise.js        seeded random numbers, hashing, simplex noise
  blocks.js       block, plant and item definitions plus fast lookup tables
  textures.js     procedural textures: blocks, plants, water normals, wings, inventory icons
  terrain.js      world generator: terrain, trees, plants, springs, spawn search
  mesher.js       chunk -> geometry (face culling, ambient occlusion, sky light)
  world.js        chunk storage and streaming, player edits, ray casting, grass spread
  water.js        water simulation, animated surface mesh and shader
  sky.js          day/night cycle, sky dome, sun and moon, shadows, fog
  nature.js       grass and flower meshes with GPU wind, pollen particles
  wildlife.js     butterflies and bees
  player.js       AABB physics, swimming, health and breath
  interaction.js  targeting, mining, placing, bucket, held item, particles
  audio.js        synthesised sound effects and ambience
```

## Design notes

- **Start-up and mouse capture.** One state machine (`loading → menu → playing ⇄ paused / inventory / dead`) and exactly one `pointerlockchange` listener. Start and Resume enter gameplay first and then request the mouse inside the same click. If the browser refuses the request (for example Chrome's short cooldown after `Esc`), the game keeps running and shows a "click to capture the mouse" hint instead of leaving a dead menu; arrow keys look around in the meantime. Hidden screens use `display: none`, so they can't catch clicks or keyboard focus.
- **Performance.** Each chunk becomes one mesh per layer (opaque, cutout, water, plants). Meshing copies the chunk and its neighbours into a bordered buffer so the inner loop never branches on chunk edges. Chunk generation, meshing and water rebuilds are time-budgeted per frame, chunks beyond the render distance are unloaded, foliage shares one material with vertex-shader wind and fades smoothly toward the vegetation limit, and the wildlife is two instanced meshes with GPU wing animation.
- **No modules, no build.** Plain classic scripts sharing one `OM` namespace, loaded in dependency order by `index.html`.

## Known limitations

- Water has no pressure — it won't climb back up a U-shaped channel.
- There are no caves yet, and leaves don't decay when you cut a tree down.
- Mined blocks go straight into your inventory instead of dropping as items.
- Saves live in `localStorage` (roughly 5 MB per site).
- Frame rate on integrated graphics may need a lower render distance or shadows turned down.
